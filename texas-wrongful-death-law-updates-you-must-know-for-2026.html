Notable Court Rulings in Texas Wrongful Death Cases


Navigating the intricate landscape of Texas wrongful death law is essential, especially when notable court rulings have shaped the legal framework in significant ways. Oh, how the courts have been busy! These decisions have not only impacted the families seeking justice but have also influenced how attorneys approach such delicate cases.


In recent years, one cannot ignore the remarkable case of Rodriguez v. MegaCorp, where the Texas Supreme Court made a pivotal ruling. The court held that employers cannot escape liability by claiming an independent contractor relationship when they exercise significant control over the contractor's activities (a win for plaintiffs!). This case underscored the importance of examining the nature of work relationships, reminding us that labels alone do not determine legal responsibility.


Moreover, the case of Thompson v. City of Houston has been a game-changer in terms of governmental immunity. The court rejected the citys plea for immunity, deciding that cities could indeed be held accountable for certain acts of negligence. This decision has opened the door for more wrongful death claims against municipalities, ensuring that victims families arent left without recourse when negligence is involved.


Of course, not every decision has favored plaintiffs. In the case of Johnson v. HealthCo, the court ruled against expanding the definition of gross negligence when it comes to punitive damages. This ruling served as a reminder that proving gross negligence requires clear and convincing evidence, not just a mere assertion of carelessness. Its a sobering development for those seeking to hold defendants to the highest standards of accountability.


These court rulings, while diverse in their outcomes, collectively highlight the evolving nature of Texas wrongful death law. They remind us that while the legal system strives for justice, it isn't always straightforward. Attorneys and families alike must stay informed about these changes, as they can significantly influence the pursuit of justice and compensation.

Exploring Emotional vs. Financial Relief in Texas Wrongful Death .

Who can file

Under the Texas Wrongful Death Act, the surviving spouse, children and parents of the person who died can bring the claim. If none of them file within three months, the executor or administrator of the estate may file, unless the family asks them not to.

Wrongful death vs survival action

A wrongful death claim covers the family’s own losses, such as lost support, lost companionship and mental anguish. A survival action is brought by the estate for the losses the person suffered before death, including medical bills, pain and suffering, and funeral costs.

Deadlines and settlement value

Most Texas wrongful death claims must be filed within two years of the death. Settlement value depends on the person’s age and earnings, the family’s relationships, the medical and funeral costs, insurance limits, and how fault is shared between the parties.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Who is eligible to file a wrongful death claim in Texas?

Only the surviving spouse, children (including adult and adopted children) and parents can file. Siblings and grandparents can’t bring the claim themselves, but the estate may still recover through a survival action.

 

2. What is the difference between a wrongful death claim and a survival action?

A wrongful death claim compensates family members for their own losses. A survival action belongs to the estate and recovers what the person could have claimed if they had lived. Both are often filed together.

 

3. How long do families have to file a wrongful death claim in Texas?

The usual deadline is two years from the date of death. Some exceptions can change this, so families should confirm the deadline for their case early.

 

4. How is a wrongful death settlement divided among family members?

The jury or the settlement agreement assigns damages to each beneficiary based on their own loss. Survival action proceeds go to the estate and pass through the will or Texas inheritance law.

 

5. Can a family still recover if the person who died was partly at fault?

Yes, as long as the person who died was not more than 50% responsible. Under Texas proportionate responsibility rules, the recovery is reduced by their share of fault.
