Legal Challenges in Pursuing a Wrongful Death Claim


When faced with the tragic loss of a loved one due to anothers negligence, Texas families might consider pursuing a wrongful death claim. However, they soon discover that this path is fraught with legal challenges. Its not a simple process, and families should be prepared for unexpected hurdles.


First and foremost, establishing liability can be complex. Not everyone is automatically liable in wrongful death cases. Families must prove that the defendants actions directly caused the death. This requires substantial evidence, which isnt always easy to gather. Eyewitness testimony, expert opinions, and detailed documentation are often necessary to build a strong case. Another challenge is the statute of limitations. In Texas, wrongful death claims must be filed within two years of the deceaseds passing. This might sound like ample time, but the emotional turmoil following a loved one's death can make this period feel incredibly short. Families must act swiftly, or else they risk losing their right to pursue compensation entirely. Dont wait too long!


Additionally, calculating damages in wrongful death cases is not straightforward. Families need to consider both economic and non-economic damages, which include medical expenses, funeral costs, lost income, and emotional suffering. But how does one put a price on a loved one's companionship and guidance? It's a daunting task, and insurance companies often exploit this ambiguity to minimize payouts.


Moreover, families often face resistance from the defendant's insurance company. They aren't eager to part with their money and frequently employ tactics to reduce liability or deny claims outright. Negotiating with them can be frustrating and time-consuming, especially without legal representation. Families might find themselves up against seasoned legal teams, which can be quite intimidating.


Finally, the emotional toll is a hidden cost that cant be overlooked. Legal battles are stressful, and reliving the circumstances of the death through depositions and court proceedings can be emotionally draining. Families must weigh the potential financial benefits against the emotional strain of a prolonged legal fight.


In conclusion, while pursuing a wrongful death claim in Texas might offer some financial relief, its not without its share of challenges. Families should prepare for a complex legal journey and consider seeking legal guidance to navigate these obstacles effectively. After all, no one should have to face such a daunting process alone, especially in their time of grief.

Who can file

Under the Texas Wrongful Death Act, the surviving spouse, children and parents of the person who died can bring the claim. If none of them file within three months, the executor or administrator of the estate may file, unless the family asks them not to.

Wrongful death vs survival action

A wrongful death claim covers the family’s own losses, such as lost support, lost companionship and mental anguish. A survival action is brought by the estate for the losses the person suffered before death, including medical bills, pain and suffering, and funeral costs.

Deadlines and settlement value

Most Texas wrongful death claims must be filed within two years of the death. Settlement value depends on the person’s age and earnings, the family’s relationships, the medical and funeral costs, insurance limits, and how fault is shared between the parties.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Who is eligible to file a wrongful death claim in Texas?

Only the surviving spouse, children (including adult and adopted children) and parents can file. Siblings and grandparents can’t bring the claim themselves, but the estate may still recover through a survival action.

 

2. What is the difference between a wrongful death claim and a survival action?

A wrongful death claim compensates family members for their own losses. A survival action belongs to the estate and recovers what the person could have claimed if they had lived. Both are often filed together.

 

3. How long do families have to file a wrongful death claim in Texas?

The usual deadline is two years from the date of death. Some exceptions can change this, so families should confirm the deadline for their case early.

 

4. How is a wrongful death settlement divided among family members?

The jury or the settlement agreement assigns damages to each beneficiary based on their own loss. Survival action proceeds go to the estate and pass through the will or Texas inheritance law.

 

5. Can a family still recover if the person who died was partly at fault?

Yes, as long as the person who died was not more than 50% responsible. Under Texas proportionate responsibility rules, the recovery is reduced by their share of fault.
