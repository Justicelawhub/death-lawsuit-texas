Understanding the Statute of Limitations for Wrongful Death Claims


Understanding the statute of limitations for wrongful death claims in Texas is a crucial step in navigating the complexities of the legal system. When a loved one passes away unexpectedly due to anothers negligence, it can be an emotional rollercoaster, and the last thing on your mind might be legal deadlines. However, its essential to be aware of these timelines to ensure that your claim isnt dismissed outright.


In Texas, the statute of limitations for filing a wrongful death claim is typically two years from the date of the deceased persons death. This might seem like a long time, but in the midst of grief and the practicalities of dealing with a loved ones passing, those months can fly by faster than youd expect! If you dont file within this period, you may lose the right to seek justice and compensation.


Its worth noting that there are exceptions to this rule. For example, if the death was caused by a government entity, different rules might apply, potentially shortening the timeframe. Additionally, if the deceaseds death was not immediately attributed to wrongful actions, the discovery rule might extend the deadline. This means that the clock starts ticking only when the wrongful nature of the death is discovered, rather than at the time of death itself.


Dont assume that these legal matters are straightforward. They are not! Wrongful death claims can be complex, and each case is unique. Therefore, its often in your best interest to consult with a legal professional who can guide you through the specifics of your case. Theyll help you understand the nuances and any potential implications of missing the deadline.


In conclusion, while dealing with the emotional aftermath of a wrongful death is undeniably challenging, understanding and adhering to the statute of limitations in Texas is vital. You wouldnt want to miss out on the opportunity for justice due to a technicality. So, take a deep breath, seek guidance, and ensure that youre taking the necessary steps to protect your rights and those of your loved one.

Primarily, the right to file lies with the deceaseds immediate family members. This typically includes the surviving spouse, children, and parents. This usually happens if none of the immediate family members have filed within three months of the death (unless they specifically request that no claim be filed).

Who can file

Under the Texas Wrongful Death Act, the surviving spouse, children and parents of the person who died can bring the claim. If none of them file within three months, the executor or administrator of the estate may file, unless the family asks them not to.

Wrongful death vs survival action

A wrongful death claim covers the family’s own losses, such as lost support, lost companionship and mental anguish. A survival action is brought by the estate for the losses the person suffered before death, including medical bills, pain and suffering, and funeral costs.

Deadlines and settlement value

Most Texas wrongful death claims must be filed within two years of the death. Settlement value depends on the person’s age and earnings, the family’s relationships, the medical and funeral costs, insurance limits, and how fault is shared between the parties.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Who is eligible to file a wrongful death claim in Texas?

Only the surviving spouse, children (including adult and adopted children) and parents can file. Siblings and grandparents can’t bring the claim themselves, but the estate may still recover through a survival action.

 

2. What is the difference between a wrongful death claim and a survival action?

A wrongful death claim compensates family members for their own losses. A survival action belongs to the estate and recovers what the person could have claimed if they had lived. Both are often filed together.

 

3. How long do families have to file a wrongful death claim in Texas?

The usual deadline is two years from the date of death. Some exceptions can change this, so families should confirm the deadline for their case early.

 

4. How is a wrongful death settlement divided among family members?

The jury or the settlement agreement assigns damages to each beneficiary based on their own loss. Survival action proceeds go to the estate and pass through the will or Texas inheritance law.

 

5. Can a family still recover if the person who died was partly at fault?

Yes, as long as the person who died was not more than 50% responsible. Under Texas proportionate responsibility rules, the recovery is reduced by their share of fault.
